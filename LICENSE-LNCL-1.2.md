## 5. Fork, Preservation, and Exit Rights

Lawful Human Users may, without additional permission:

- preserve complete copies of versions they lawfully receive;
- mirror repositories and release artifacts;
- maintain independent package or artifact archives;
- self-host;
- remove network dependencies;
- continue abandoned versions;
- create independent forks;
- replace maintainers or governance in their own forks;
- migrate their own data away from a deployment they control.

### 5.1 Fork Obligations

Any fork or Covered Work that is distributed to another party must:

1. retain this License for all Covered portions;
2. include the complete text of this License;
3. retain applicable copyright and attribution notices;
4. identify material modifications;
5. preserve provenance information where reasonably practical;
6. identify the distributed fork as Source-Available or Binary-Only where reasonably practical;
7. if distributed as Source-Available, make corresponding source code available to recipients;
8. avoid falsely implying official status, endorsement, or continuity with the original project.

**The right to fork does not include the right to erase provenance.**

A fork may change its own name, governance, architecture, maintainers, roadmap, interfaces, distribution model, and project direction, provided the Covered portions remain subject to this License and attribution obligations.

No repository, package registry, hosting platform, maintainer, or service is promised to remain permanently available.
